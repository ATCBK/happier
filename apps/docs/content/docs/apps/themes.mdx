Happier ships 18 built-in themes and lets you build your own from any of them.
Colours carry everywhere the app draws — surfaces, text, controls, diffs, syntax
highlighting, the embedded editors, and the native system chrome around the
window — so a theme is not just the chat background.

Find them under **Settings → Appearance**.

## Layout (0.3 development)

The unreleased 0.3 client adds **Session first** and **Editor first** under
**Settings → Appearance → Layout** on wider devices. Session and Project pages
follow this device-local preference; Workflow and app pages keep their current
order. Switching preserves open content, drafts and scroll positions. The title
strip, keyboard shortcuts and command palette offer the same arrangement choice.

When a page has another column beside it, its header can hide the main column.
A restore chip keeps the page's identity and live state visible and brings it
back. Navigation stays independent. Drag a column's divider to resize it;
double-click resets its default width. Collection rails remember their width on
this device and remain visible when dragged to their minimum. See
[Files and the editor](/code/files-and-editor) for the session workflow.

## Built-in themes

The presets cover the usual dark and light families plus several ports of
editor themes people already use: Crisp, Pitch, Sunset, Night, Graphite and
Classic on the dark side, Crisp and Paper on the light side, and Tokyo Night,
One Dark Pro, Monokai Pro, GitHub Dark and Light, Dark Modern, and the four
Catppuccin flavours.

Built-in themes are read-only. To change one, clone it — the clone is yours to
edit and the original stays intact.

## Making your own

A custom theme starts from a preset, so you are adjusting a coherent palette
rather than assembling one from nothing. From there you can override individual
colour tokens, grouped by what they affect rather than by variable name.

Two things worth knowing while editing:

- **The preview is a live sandbox.** It shows surfaces, text, controls, state
  colours and syntax together, so you can judge a change against the parts of
  the app you do not currently have open.
- **Changing the preset replaces your draft colours.** Happier asks first,
  because unsaved edits are discarded when the starting point changes.

A theme also declares whether it uses light or dark app assets, which is what
keeps icons and images legible against the palette you chose.

## Moving themes around

Export a theme to JSON and import it on another device, or share it. The export
is versioned, so a file made today keeps working as the token set grows.

Import also accepts **VS Code themes**, which is usually the fastest way to make
Happier match the editor you already stare at all day. Imports are validated
rather than trusted: a malformed file is rejected with a reason instead of
producing a half-applied palette.

## Surface finish (0.3 development)

In the 0.3 development client, **Settings → Appearance → Surface finish** offers
**Flat** and **Soft**. Soft is the default: a gentle gradient gives cards,
floating surfaces, the composer and buttons a little depth. Flat keeps their
existing borders and raised edges. Fields, rows and chips keep their flat fill.

Expand **Customize surfaces** to choose a finish for each role. **Auto** follows
the global choice. Card-framed widgets follow the card choice; plain widgets
keep their unframed presentation. These preferences are saved on this device.

Soft works over glass too, preserving the backdrop through its tint or blur.
In dark themes, its top light takes the card surface's hue and uses a gentler
strength. Light themes keep their existing finish.
In a custom theme, the **Soft finish** colour controls the ink and its alpha
controls the strength. Transparent removes the gradient.

## Glass (0.3 development)

In the 0.3 development client, floating menus, popovers, dialogs, sheets,
tooltips and toasts use clear glass by default whenever glass is enabled.
**Auto**, **Everywhere** and **Clear** share that floating material; their
surrounding chrome, sidebar and content still have different coats.

The presets are quick choices above five layers: **Window** (the app over your
desktop), **Title bar and rail**, **Sidebar**, **Elements** and **Menus and dialogs**.
Each layer offers **Glass** or **Solid**, with one global blur strength. Window
glass needs the desktop app; a browser cannot show your desktop.

Elements covers cards, widgets, the composer, settings groups and the sidebar's
rounded list groups. Its default **Glass** choice shows glass when a backdrop
or a translucent window is behind it. Without either, it keeps its usual solid
fill. Changing Elements keeps the selected preset, and switching presets
preserves your Elements choice. Changing another layer makes the preset **Custom**.
On phones, glass Elements uses translucent tint.

On the web, glass surfaces use 24px frost. **Settings › Appearance › Glass and backdrop ›
Tint** sets how much colour menus, dialogs, cards and the composer add over
what is behind them: Subtle (the default) is almost pure blur with a hair of
tint, Medium shows each surface's own fill, and Strong is clearly frosted.
Over the plain page, glass never looks lighter or greyer than the page it sits
on. Over a background picture, the picture is dimmed only enough to keep
primary text readable, and keeps its colour. Light mode keeps glass close to
white. Secondary text over very bright, saturated pictures can fall below its
contrast target; this is an accepted limit of the clear material. Tint applies
to the named presets; custom materials keep their exact values.
This development change is not an availability claim for released clients.
It does not turn a bright or dark page into a uniform fill. Native
clients use Liquid Glass where available, or their existing tinted blur.
**Solid** and the system's **Reduce Transparency** setting remove translucency;
an unavailable native material also falls back to solid paint. Custom per-group
opacity and blur are left exactly as you set them.

## Home backdrop (0.3 development)

Home, new sessions and projects can show a backdrop behind the page: our dot
planet (the default, in the top-right corner), a picture, or nothing. Open
**Look** from the chip at the top right of Home or from **Customize → Look**;
on a phone it opens as a sheet. Every choice applies as you make it and follows
your account to your other devices.

- **Planet**: choose where it sits (**Corner**, **Side** or **Rise**; phones
  show Rise as Corner), its style (**Airy**, **Balanced** or **Rich**) and
  whether faint typed digits surround it.
- **Picture**: choose one of the built-in photos and paintings (each is
  credited under the thumbnails), **Shuffle**, or **Your picture…** to add one
  of your own. Built-in pictures download from their source the first time they
  are shown and are then kept on the device. Your own picture stays on the
  device you added it on; other devices show the default backdrop instead.
- **Look**, **Fade** and **Strength** change how the picture is drawn. Each
  picture remembers your changes; **Reset to this picture's look** returns it
  to the look it ships with. Fade chooses **Ordered**, **Gradient**, **Haze**,
  **Shrink** or **Rows**; Strength mixes the look from 0% to 100%.
- **Layout**: **Horizon** draws the picture as a band behind the greeting and
  composer; **Wallpaper** fills the window. The backdrop and glass are independent:
  Wallpaper keeps that layout with every glass setting, including **Solid** and
  Reduce Transparency. The title bar, sidebar, cards and composer stay opaque
  under Solid and Reduce Transparency while the backdrop remains visible around them.
- **Glass**: the same glass material and intensity as above.

With a built-in picture, the chip at the top right of Home shows the app's
version and the picture's name (a phone shows only the version). Press it for
the picture's credit and a link to its source, **What's new in** this version
(the release notes), and **Backdrop**, which opens Look. The **Release caption**
moment switch turns this off and leaves only the picture's name.

**Settings → Appearance → Glass and backdrop** has the same controls, a preview
of your Home in the glass tiles, whether the backdrop also shows behind
sessions (off by default), and a switch for each of the backdrop's moments.

## Loading indicator

The spinner that shows while an agent works has its own picker under
**Settings → Appearance → Loading Indicator**. Eleven styles draw the H of
Happier in dots and move light through it in different ways: Wave (the
default), Handwritten, Build and Release, Relay, Twin Stems, Slow Breath,
Starfield, Sweep, Radar, Ripple and Aurora. **Classic Ring** keeps the original
rotating ring. Each option in the picker is a live preview.

Two more choices sit under the picker. **Speed** (Slow, Normal or Fast) sets
how quickly the light moves through the dots. **Pause** (None, Short or Long)
sets how long the dots rest before each loop starts again; Short is the
default. The pause stays the same length at every speed. Styles that loop
without resting, such as Slow Breath, Starfield, Radar and Aurora, ignore the
pause, and the Classic Ring keeps its own pace, so the settings that do not
apply are greyed out with a note saying why. The previews play at your chosen
speed and pause.

Spinners follow your theme's text colour. Aurora uses the theme's indigo,
purple and orange accents instead, except on tinted surfaces such as a filled
button, where it falls back to that surface's colour so it stays readable.

With your system's Reduce Motion setting on, no light travels across the
indicator: the dots hold still and fade gently in and out, and the Classic Ring
stops turning. Spinners also hold still while Happier's window or tab is hidden.
These choices are saved per device. Plugin surfaces always use Wave at the
default speed and pause.
